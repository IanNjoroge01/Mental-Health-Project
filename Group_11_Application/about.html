About the Mental Health Statistics App

This Shiny app provides an interactive exploration of global mental health statistics. It aims to present key insights into the prevalence, impact, and trends of mental health conditions worldwide.

Purpose

The goal of this app is to provide easy access to mental health data, helping researchers, policymakers, and individuals understand the burden of mental health disorders across different regions.

Note on IHME Data

It is important to note that the IHME dataset reports only 231 deaths attributed to mental health disorders, which is significantly lower than the true global burden of mental health. This discrepancy is likely due to variations in how deaths are classified and reported across countries, and the challenge of accurately capturing the wide range of conditions that can contribute to mental health-related mortality.

We also noted that the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study 2019 found that deaths and Years of Life Lost (YLLs) is only for anorexia nervosa which is classified as eating disorders under mental health disorders, as these were the only mental disorders identified as underlying causes of death in that study. This suggests that mortality directly attributed to mental disorders is relatively low.

Given more time we can explore other sources that may have statistics on other mental health disorders besides eating disorders.

Burden

Burden of disease studies estimate the population-wide impact of living with diseaseand injury and dying prematurely. They involve calculations using the Disability-Adjusted LifeYear (DALY), where one DALY represents the loss of one year of full health. DALYs combine in one measure the years of life lost to premature mortality (YLLs) and years of healthy life lost to disability (YLDs) to estimate the overall burden from each cause of disease and injury.

IHME-GDB Metrics

Instead of downloading pre-aggregated data, we resolved to download the full dataset with all granular details (sex, age, location) and then aggregate using R. The dataset provides three metric types:
  • Number: Absolute counts (e.g., total deaths, total cases) which can be summed.
  • Percent: Proportion of total which equires a weighted average.
  • Rate: Cases per 100,000 people Which Requires a weighted average.

Appearance - Theme

We chose to work with custom styling (css/scss) as opposed to shiny themes. This gives us control in case users recommend their brand (corporate) colours we can easily customize.
